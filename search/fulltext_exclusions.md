## The reporting subset

Of these 40, **eight report a quantified GWP reduction for a carbonation-treated cement-based
product but fail I2** — entries 1, 2, 10, 23, 24, 25, 33 and 37. Together with the 17 included
studies, that is **25 papers reporting an environmental result for a carbonation-treated
cement product, of which 8 do not declare both a functional unit and a cradle-to-gate
boundary.**

Entry 34 is a further case of the same kind, and the starkest: a paper whose **title** claims
carbon footprint reduction and which contains no life-cycle assessment of any sort. It is
excluded under X3 rather than counted among the seven, because it makes no quantified
environmental claim to assess — but a practitioner searching on title alone would not know
that.

Entry 1 and entry 26 of the *included* set share their first two authors: Noshin and Abbass
published both the *J. Clean. Prod.* paper excluded here for declaring no functional unit and
the *Clean. Waste Syst.* paper included as study 11, which declares a functional unit of 1 kg,
a cradle-to-gate boundary, openLCA v2.4 and ReCiPe 2016. The same group reports to two
different standards in the same year.

## A fourth form of incommensurability

Entry 40 is worth separating from the rest. Sections 4.3 and 7.5 describe three respects in
which the retrieved outcomes fail to share an axis — functional unit, comparator, and the
structural form of the reported result. This study adds a fourth: **the impact-assessment
output itself.** It performs a competent LCA of a CO₂-cured product against non-carbonated
comparators, at a functional unit better suited to structural comparison than any unit in the
included set, and reports the answer as a weighted single score. Nothing in it can be placed on
a GWP axis, not because the work is weak but because the reporting convention differs.
